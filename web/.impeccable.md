## Design Context

### Users

DCI Wellness serves three equally important audiences:

1. **Individuals & families** in Uganda seeking mental wellness tools — mood trackers, journals, card games, and the full toolkit. They arrive looking for something tangible they can use today, often after a moment of "I need to do something about this." Context: many are mobile-first, some have limited connectivity, and the topic itself carries stigma. They need to feel safe before they'll act.

2. **Organisational decision-makers** — NGO directors, school heads, corporate HR, faith community leaders — evaluating DCI as a program or partner. They need to see credibility (40+ years of founder experience, institutional affiliations), clear pricing, and measurable impact (Connection Miles). They're comparing DCI to clinical services and need to understand it's complementary, not competing.

3. **Community leaders** who want to become Community Champions or run Conversation Clinics. They're mission-driven, looking for structure and training. They need to feel like this is real infrastructure, not a volunteer hobby.

The site's job: move all three audiences from curiosity to action — buying a toolkit, booking a call, or applying for training — without making any audience feel the site isn't for them.

### Brand Personality

**Warm, grounded, honest.**

- **Warm:** Not clinical, not institutional. The voice of someone who's been there — Emily's "I built three ventures while burning out" energy. Conversational but not casual. No jargon walls.
- **Grounded:** Rooted in Uganda, in community, in 40 years of real work. Not aspirational Silicon Valley wellness. Earthy, tactile, physical (the toolkit is a real box you hold). The DCI head motif: "No hair. No gender. No name. Before the labels, we're all just people trying to be okay."
- **Honest:** The crisis-origin narrative doesn't flinch. "Part after part is all fun, until it isn't." "Why didn't I see it coming?" Direct copy that earns trust through specificity, not through softening.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone:** Modern product-forward clarity (inspired by [wisprflow.ai](https://wisprflow.ai/)) adapted to DCI's warm, organic palette. Clean layouts and generous whitespace, but textured rather than sterile — subtle dot patterns, noise overlays, hand-drawn SVG flourishes. The effect should feel like holding a well-made physical object: considered, tactile, intentional.

**Palette:**
- Deep teal (#1A3C40, #2A6268) — trust, depth, the "infrastructure" feeling
- Burgundy (#6F231E) — warmth, urgency, the accent that draws the eye
- Cream/sand/paper (#F6ECD9, #EFE5D0, #FFF9EA) — safety, softness, the "room you feel welcome in"
- Clay (#D49C70) — earthiness, warmth, African soil

**Typography:**
- Headings: Fraunces (weight 500-700) — editorial, grounded, serious without being cold
- Body: Outfit (400-600) — clean, modern, highly readable at small sizes

**Shape language:** Rounded corners (1.5-3rem on cards, 2rem+ on sections), floating elements with soft shadows, subtle animation (reveal-on-scroll, gentle float). Nothing sharp or clinical.

**Theme:** Both light and dark modes. Light mode is the canonical brand expression (cream/sand backgrounds, teal/burgundy accents). Dark mode should feel like a quiet room at night — the same warmth expressed through deep teals and muted cream accents, not a generic dark inversion.

**References:**
- [wisprflow.ai](https://wisprflow.ai/) — product-forward clarity, clean component design, confident spacing
- The founder's live site (deeperconnectioninitiative.com) — copy tone, content structure, product hierarchy

**Anti-references:**
- Generic "wellness app" pastels (Headspace-style illustration overload)
- Clinical / hospital aesthetics
- Overly corporate NGO sites with stock photography
- Anything that treats mental health as a luxury lifestyle product

### Accessibility & Inclusion

This is a mental health startup. Accessibility is not compliance — it's care.

- **WCAG AA minimum** across all interactive elements. Aim higher where practical.
- **Crisis-safe design:** No content that could trigger distress without warning. The crisis-origin narrative (Stage 4) should be presented with care — adequate spacing, no autoplay, no jarring transitions.
- **Reduced motion:** All animations respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (already implemented via CSS media query). Float and reveal animations disable cleanly.
- **Colour contrast:** The cream-on-teal and cream-on-burgundy combinations must meet AA ratios. Test all text-on-brand-colour combinations.
- **Keyboard navigation:** Full keyboard access for all interactive elements, including the mega-menu, mobile sheet, and any card interactions.
- **Screen reader support:** Proper ARIA labels, semantic HTML, descriptive link text (no "click here").
- **Cognitive accessibility:** Clear visual hierarchy, short paragraphs, consistent navigation patterns. The site serves people who may be in emotional distress — it should never feel overwhelming.
- **Mobile-first:** Many users are on mobile devices in Uganda. Touch targets must be 44px minimum. The site must be usable on slower connections.
- **Offline consideration:** The physical toolkit is offline-first by design. The site should load fast and degrade gracefully.

### Design Principles

1. **Safety before persuasion.** Every page should feel like a room you'd want to stay in. Earn trust through warmth and honesty, not through pressure. The emotional arc is: safety, then hope, then trust.

2. **Tangible over abstract.** DCI's strength is physical, real things — a box you hold, a card you flip, a journal you write in, a room you sit in. Design should make the digital feel grounded: real product photography over illustrations, texture over flat colour, weight over float.

3. **Community, not consumer.** This is not a product catalogue. Every toolkit sold feeds an ecosystem. Every Connection Mile represents a real life impacted. Design should always connect individual action to collective impact.

4. **Clarity is compassion.** People visiting a mental health site may be in distress, uncertain, or stigma-wary. Clear CTAs, honest pricing, plain language, and predictable navigation are acts of care. Never make someone work to find help.

5. **African, not imported.** The visual identity should feel rooted in Uganda — earthy tones, organic textures, community-oriented layouts. Not a Western wellness template with African content pasted in. The cream/teal/burgundy palette is the soil this grows from.
